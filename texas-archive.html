Sponsor money was systematically under-detected

One counter-rule: when a company's own recent release claims private family ownership and an aggregator claims a PE round, the primary source wins pending a call — see Massey. Ghost rows and donor tracing
Seven rows named companies that do not exist in Texas. Tracing each to the company it was scraped from turns a dead row into a diagnosis:

Donorless — fabrications. Cast Products and Segovia Distributing have no identifiable donor at all. That absence is diagnostic: an import leaves a traceable source, so a ghost with none was invented at build time. Different defect, and the worse of the two.

Small private manufacturers: four of six rows bad. The thinnest public footprint and the most generic names in the file. Any future market build should treat sub-$50M manufacturers as requiring primary-source confirmation before the row is written, not after.

The ones that consolidated produced the file's worst row: Block Distributing merged into Republic two decades ago, became a founding pillar of RNDC in 2007, and RNDC is now in Chapter 11 with its Texas operations sold to Reyes. A company that stopped existing independently before the iPhone, whose successor then collapsed.
