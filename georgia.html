Taxonomy additions from this market (carry forward to the remaining ten states):

  • GA-suffix / bare-state-suffix pre-check — promote to permanent pre-check status. Any row whose name is a national brand plus a bare state abbreviation ("X GA," "X Michigan," etc.) is a probable satellite of an out-of-state parent until proven otherwise. This pass alone caught JE Dunn GA, McCarthy GA, Metromont GA, Tindall Corporation GA, Consolidated Pipe GA, Coburn Supply GA, Rick Hendrick GA, Milliken GA, Brasfield & Gorrie GA, Dutch Farms GA, Birdsong Peanuts GA, and Cook's Pest Control GA — twelve for twelve. Several (JE Dunn, Rick Hendrick, Milliken) stacked out-of-state with scale for a double disqualifier straight to C; the rest carried genuine family ownership out-of-state and landed on B.
  • Operator-retained, owner-replaced (new, distinct class) — Empire Distributors. Berkshire Hathaway/McLane bought the parent in 2010; the Kahn family stayed on as President/CEO and the "family-owned, family-operated" marketing never stopped, fifteen years later. This is not staleness (a snapshot that aged) — it's a maintained narrative sitting on top of a real, durable ownership change. The people are real and reachable; they don't approve the budget. Distinguish this from the standard "acquired, family stayed as employees" pattern by how deliberately and how long the family-ownership language has been sustained after the fact.
  • Duplicate-parent catch, single-market instance — Gwinnett Place Honda (#137) is a Hendrick Automotive Group location, the same ultimate owner as Rick Hendrick GA (#130) elsewhere in this same file. The cross-market duplicate-parent principle applies just as well within one market's 200 rows; the six-pre-check token scan should watch for it both ways.
  • RNDC Chapter 11 — freshest catch of the project, and it closes a loop. National Distributing Company's affiliate Republic National Distributing Company filed Chapter 11 weeks before this file was built, after already divesting Colorado, Oregon, and Washington. This is the same collapse Chicago's Block Distributing row traced from the acquirer's side — two markets, one failing company, approached from opposite ends of the country.
  • Positive-direction recency catch — Waffle House. Every recency catch so far has run one direction: family-owned company quietly loses its family door. Waffle House ran the other way — longtime professional CEO Walt Ehmer died in office (Sept 2024), and the seat passed back to 3rd-generation Joe Rogers III rather than to another outside executive. Recency-weighted search needs to catch reversions to family control, not just exits from it.
  • Dalton flooring corridor confirmed as predicted, but already priced in. Heavy concentration (Shaw, Mohawk, Interface, Dixie Group, Tarkett, Oriental Weavers) was already correctly Tier C on arrival.
